**1. Figure 4 scope, presentation, and directed-edge validation.**
Figure 4 was intended as a focused evaluation within a four-ROI tongue-motion network, not an unconstrained whole-brain discovery analysis. The ROIs were defined from data-specific FSL FEAT activation maps (Appendix C.1). Among the examined HCP motor conditions, tongue motion showed the strongest departures from Gaussianity (Fig. 2B) while engaging a task-relevant network with established contralateral cerebello-cortical circuitry (Manto et al., 2012; Sasegbon et al., 2023) and bilateral orofacial sensorimotor organization (Rizzolatti et al., 2001; Todorov et al., 2004). Dual-flow identified a task-sensitive directed contralateral cerebello-cortical interaction and bidirectional interactions between the bilateral orofacial sensorimotor regions, consistent with these known circuit organizations. Only three of the 12 candidate directed effective-connectivity (EC) pairs satisfied the tongue-versus-rest significance criterion after FDR correction and the between-subject variability criterion (Appendix C.3). The sparse result therefore reflects the restricted network and statistical filtering rather than an expectation of dense connectivity.

We agree that the current Fig. 4 does not facilitate cross-method interpretation. Appendix Fig. 6 already compares the same tongue-motion analysis across methods by rendering the estimated directed connections between anatomically localized ROIs. We will replace the current Fig. 4 with an expanded version of Appendix Fig. 6 showing Dual-flow, Gaussian Capacity (GCap), Granger Causality (GC), and VAR-LiNGAM side by side, and move the current EC matrices and significance markers to the appendix. The starred-edge criteria are already defined in Appendix C.3; we will add a direct caption cross-reference. GIMME is not included in this short-window HCP data analysis because its group-level SEM search is not directly applicable to these short task segments, but it is evaluated in the longer known-edge simulations.

The real-data analysis evaluates task-sensitive, model-based directed interactions consistent with tongue-motion circuitry; it does not provide ground-truth validation of the exact directed edges. We therefore added controlled BOLD simulations with prespecified ground-truth directed EC matrices, including chain, diamond, and observed common-driver topologies. These simulations test recovery of known directed interactions under fixed ground-truth EC structures, rather than detection of distributional changes alone. Across four conditions with 50 realizations each, Dual-flow achieved the highest average precision (0.778) and the highest mean AUROC and sensitivity in every condition, relative to GCap, GC, VAR-LiNGAM, and GIMME (see our response to Reviewer 3TGk, Point 2 and the corresponding table). Together, the expanded main-text comparison and known-edge simulations assess when distribution-aware capacity estimation differs from or outperforms Gaussian-capacity and other EC estimators.

**2. Estimator degrees of freedom and parameter sensitivity.**
Model complexity was partly data-adaptive. For the short-window tongue-fMRI analysis, FIR order was selected separately for each directed ROI pair and subject using AICc, yielding $N_h=1.61\pm1.52$ samples (range 1–10).

We also evaluated DualNet sequence length $T$ in a separate controlled experiment using the simulator described in the first paragraph of Section 4.3. We examined $T\in${256, 512, 1024, 2048, 4096}, 50 random initializations, and four residual-noise distributions, i.e., Gaussian, Uniform, Exponential, and Student’s $t$, yielding 1,000 runs. Mean capacity differed from the default $T$=1024 by at most 4.30%, and all shifts were no larger than the corresponding SD across random seeds; all runs produced finite estimates. Thus, capacity estimation was stable over the examined sequence-length range (see our response to Reviewer 3TGk, Point 4 and the corresponding table).

In addition, GCap provides a direct within-framework ablation: it retains the same directional FIR modeling and edge-selection procedure but replaces empirical residual modeling and input-distribution optimization with Gaussian channel capacity. The comparison of Dual-flow with GCap therefore isolates the contribution of distribution-aware capacity estimation more directly than comparisons with structurally different EC methods. The Real-NVP architecture with three coupling-layer pairs and the EMA-based convergence criterion were fixed across experiments rather than tuned to individual datasets. A broader architecture-depth ablation was not performed and will be acknowledged as a limitation.

**3. Robustness of the Figure 4 edges.**
For the Figure 4 dataset, we assessed robustness across subjects. The first tongue-motion trial and its corresponding resting-fixation period from acquisition run 1 were analyzed consistently across all 44 subjects. Candidate edges were evaluated using paired tongue-versus-rest tests, FDR correction across the 12 directed pairs, and a prespecified coefficient-of-variation criterion for between-subject variability (Appendix C.3). Only three edges satisfied all criteria.

We did not evaluate run-to-run or seed-level repeatability of these specific real-data edges and therefore do not claim such stability. Random-initialization sensitivity was assessed separately using the controlled residual-distribution experiments described in Point 2, rather than on the Figure 4 dataset. Estimator accuracy under fixed ground-truth EC structures was evaluated separately using the 50-realization known-edge BOLD simulations described in Point 1. Thus, the real-data analysis assesses across-subject robustness under the prespecified statistical criteria, whereas initialization sensitivity and directed-edge recovery were evaluated under controlled conditions.
