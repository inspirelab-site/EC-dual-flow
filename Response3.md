**Channel capacity as an EC metric.**
For each ordered ROI pair X->Y, we fit a directional FIR model and quantify the capacity of the resulting FIR-residual channel. Capacity therefore measures the maximum information rate supported by the fitted \(X\to Y\) dynamics under the specified input-power constraint and empirical residual variability. This interpretation assumes that the pairwise FIR model adequately captures the relevant directed dynamics and that residual variability is approximately stationary and additive within each analyzed segment. It is a model-based measure of directed dependence, not proof of a direct anatomical or causal connection: hidden common drivers or omitted regions can produce elevated pairwise capacity without a direct edge. We will clarify these assumptions and avoid interpreting capacity as definitive neural causation. To directly examine this failure mode, we added common-driver simulations containing 1->2 and 1->3, but no 2<-->3 connection, under equal and unequal coupling strengths. These results quantify recovery and false-positive behavior in the presence of a shared driver.
