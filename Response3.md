**1. Channel capacity as a novel information-theoretic, model-based effective-connectivity measure.**
Effective connectivity (EC) is inherently model-based: different approaches define method-specific measures of directed interaction under different assumptions (Roebroeck et al., NeuroImage, 2005; Valdés-Sosa et al., NeuroImage, 2011; Deshpande and Hu, Brain Connectivity, 2012; Bielczyk et al., Network Neuroscience, 2019). Smith et al. (2011), for example, evaluated diverse network-modeling methods with fundamentally different outputs by their ability to recover known edges and directions from simulated fMRI. Following this established practice, we do not claim that channel capacity is a universal or model-independent “strength” of EC, nor merely a coupling coefficient. We propose it as a distinct information-theoretic EC measure and evaluate it through directed-edge recovery. 

Brain-signal and residual distributions are not nuisance details; they directly shape the information-carrying limit of a fitted directional interaction. Yet this distributional structure is rarely incorporated into model-based EC metrics. For each ordered ROI pair X->Y, we fit a directional FIR model, interpret the resulting FIR–residual system as a noisy communication channel, and use its capacity as the EC measure. Capacity quantifies the maximum information rate supported by the fitted channel under an input-power constraint and therefore depends jointly on its directional dynamics, residual distribution, and input distribution. GCap provides our Gaussian formulation, whereas Dual-flow learns the empirical residual distribution and optimizes the input distribution without assuming Gaussianity.

We agree that a shared upstream state can elevate pairwise capacity even when two regions lack a direct connection. We therefore added common-driver simulations in which X->Y and X->Z, with no Y<-->Z edge, under equal (0.4/0.4) and unequal (0.8/0.4) coupling strengths. In the pairwise Y-Z analysis, X is omitted and therefore acts as an unmodeled shared driver. Dual-flow achieved mean AUROCs of .925±.186 and .910±.168, sensitivities of .900±.226 and .790±.249, and false-positive rates of .070±.152 and .075±.136 under the equal- and unequal-coupling conditions, respectively (see full table in our response to Reviewer 3TGk, Point 2). These simulations directly test the reviewer’s proposed common-driver scenario.

Channel capacity has previously been used to characterize information limits in single neurons and neuronal circuits (MacKay and McCulloch, 1952; Ikeda and Manton, 2009; Shew et al., 2011; Barral et al., 2019). Building on this literature, our novelty is to formulate the capacity of a fitted directional interaction between brain regions as a model-based EC measure. To our knowledge, channel capacity has not previously been formulated for static or time-resolved interregional EC. It may therefore characterize distribution-dependent communication potential beyond coupling magnitude alone. In this study, the channel is instantiated by a pairwise FIR–residual model within short windows; extension to multivariate autoregressive or other dynamical channel models is a future direction. Because the resulting capacity is conditional on the selected model and observed variables, it should not be interpreted as proof of a direct anatomical connection, established neural causation, or the amount of information actually transmitted in vivo. Before this metric can be translated into a clinically actionable biomarker, rigorous disease-specific validation in independent cohorts will be essential.

**2. Clarification relative to existing non-Gaussian methods and broader EC baselines.**
We apologize that the original Introduction did not clearly distinguish our contribution from established non-Gaussian causal methods such as LiNGAM. LiNGAM-family methods exploit non-Gaussian disturbances to identify causal structure under specific independence and structural assumptions. Our framework uses distributional information differently: we characterize departures from Gaussianity in neural signals and fitted residuals and incorporate the empirical residual distribution directly into a channel-capacity EC measure while optimizing the admissible input distribution.

Because the tongue-motion analysis does not provide exact directed-edge ground truth, we added controlled BOLD simulations with prespecified chain, diamond, and common-driver structures. We selected representative, well-established EC baselines spanning complementary frameworks that remain in current neuroimaging use and can be evaluated on the same ROI time series and directed ground truth. GC measures directed predictability, VAR-LiNGAM uses non-Gaussianity for structural identification, and GIMME performs group- and individual-level SEM search; recent applications and benchmarks demonstrate their continued relevance (Zhu et al., 2023; Mellema and Montillo, 2023; Arab et al., 2025; Murray et al., 2024). In contrast, our framework uses the capacity of a fitted directional FIR–residual channel as the EC metric. GCap is the within-framework Gaussian formulation, whereas Dual-flow models the empirical residual distribution and optimizes the admissible input distribution. The full baseline rationale and quantitative comparison are provided in our response to Reviewer 3TGk, Point 2. In summary, across four conditions with 50 realizations each, Dual-flow achieved the highest mean AUROC and sensitivity in every condition and the highest average precision across conditions (.778).

We also added VAR-LiNGAM to the expanded tongue-motion comparison. GIMME is evaluated in the longer known-edge simulations but not in the short-window HCP analysis because its group-level SEM search is not directly applicable to these short task segments. We will revise the Introduction and Related Work to distinguish our use of non-Gaussianity from LiNGAM, add recent applications and benchmarks of established EC frameworks, and discuss newer approaches that are not directly comparable because they require different data, priors, or experimental inputs.

**3. Insight provided by channel capacity beyond a conventional EC coefficient.**
Channel capacity is not simply a re-expression of an FIR coefficient or binary edge decision. It characterizes how fitted directional dynamics and residual uncertainty jointly constrain the information-carrying capability of the modeled interaction. Importantly, we evaluated whether time-resolved EC estimated from LFP band-limited power by each method recovered the BOLD–neural coupling previously identified using sliding-window correlation in comparable concurrent rat BOLD–LFP data. For each frequency band, we correlated the method-specific BLP-derived EC sequence with the BOLD sliding-window correlation sequence. Both GCap and Dual-flow showed significant positive correlations in theta, low-beta, high-beta, and gamma bands, whereas VAR-LiNGAM and GC showed no significant correlations in any band. This higher-frequency profile is consistent with the coupling pattern reported by Thompson et al. (NeuroImage, 2013, Fig. 2A), providing cross-modal physiological evidence that time-resolved capacity captures neural dynamics beyond directed-edge recovery. We will include the complete analysis and figure in Appendix D of the revision.

**4. Accuracy of the empirical residual-pool approximation.**
The true residual distribution is unavailable in real neural data. We therefore approximate it nonparametrically by uniformly resampling with replacement from the empirical residual pool obtained after fitting each directional FIR model. This preserves the observed marginal residual distribution without imposing a Gaussian or other parametric family.

The controlled experiments provide direct validation because the true residual distributions are known. As shown in Fig. 3, capacity estimates obtained using empirical residual resampling closely matched the corresponding theoretical capacities under the tested Gaussian, uniform, exponential, and Student’s-$t$ settings. We additionally evaluated five sequence lengths, $T\in${256, 512, 1024, 2048, 4096}, across 50 random seeds and four residual distributions, yielding 1,000 runs. Mean capacity differed from the default T=1024 by at most 4.30%, all shifts were no larger than the corresponding SD across random seeds, and all runs produced finite estimates (see our response to Reviewer 3TGk, Point 4 and the corresponding table). We will add this justification and explicitly discuss that the approximation depends on residual-pool size, representativeness, and approximate stationarity within the analyzed segment.
