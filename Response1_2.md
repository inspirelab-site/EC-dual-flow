Thank you for the additional comments. We understand the validity concern to encompass two related issues: (i) the reliability of independently estimated pairwise EC, including variability across simulation realizations and spatial consistency across heterogeneous neuroanatomical regions at the whole-brain scale; and (ii) generalization from the current 3–5-ROI networks to larger networks with additional topological complexity. We address these validity issues and computational scalability separately below.

**1. Validity: reliability of pairwise EC and generalization to complex networks**

- **Reliability across simulation realizations.** We agree that the variability in the common-driver conditions warrants careful interpretation. Each three-node network contains only two true directed edges and six candidate directed pairs. Consequently, one missed edge changes realization-level sensitivity by 0.5, and the other threshold-dependent metrics have similarly coarse resolution. Nevertheless, across 50 independent realizations, Dual-flow achieved mean AUROCs of .925 and .910, sensitivities of .900 and .790, and condition-level FPRs of .070 and .075 in the equal- and unequal-coupling common-driver conditions, respectively. We will clarify the discrete nature of these realization-level metrics and supplement mean±SD values with confidence intervals or aggregate recovery statistics.

- **Spatial reliability and regional hemodynamic heterogeneity.** Although heterogeneous regional hemodynamics were not explicitly manipulated in the current simulations, prior whole-brain work using the same pairwise FIR-based temporal model with a simpler connectivity readout analyzed 333 ROIs. The FIR order—interpreted as the duration of information transfer—was selected independently for every ordered ROI pair through data-driven model selection, without imposing a common temporal-response length or spatial-symmetry constraint. Nevertheless, the resulting duration estimates exhibited bilaterally organized hubs together with coherent ROI- and network-specific variation ([Xu et al.](https://lbc-spreng.netlify.app/uploads/Xu_etal_NI_2021.pdf), *NeuroImage*, 2021, Figs. 3 and 5). More recent multimodal work using the same FIR-selected duration measure found that duration was more susceptible than connectivity strength to temporally extended BOLD responses and regional hemodynamic timing ([Xu et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13060345/), *bioRxiv*, 2026). These findings indicate that independently fitted pairwise FIR models can capture spatially structured temporal heterogeneity related to hemodynamic timing rather than necessarily producing arbitrary pairwise variation.

  We emphasize that this prior evidence does not constitute ground-truth recovery of region-specific HRFs and does not replace direct validation of the present Dual-flow capacity readout under simulated heterogeneous hemodynamics. It does, however, provide relevant evidence that the shared pairwise FIR backbone can yield spatially coherent, data-driven temporal estimates at whole-brain scale.

- **Realistic network topology.** We agree that the current simulations do not encompass every feature of realistically sized brain networks. We distinguish between the complexities already evaluated and those that remain untested:

  - **Complexities already evaluated—indirect paths and hidden common inputs.** The chain and diamond networks contain multiple indirect pathways. In both common-driver conditions, X causes Y and Z but is deliberately omitted when fitting Y→Z and Z→Y. These conditions therefore test whether the pairwise estimator produces spurious directed connections when the fitted pair shares an unobserved common driver.

  - **Complexities not yet evaluated—feedback, modularity, and larger network scale.** Feedback cycles and explicit modular organization were not included in the added simulations. Larger recurrent and modular networks therefore remain important targets for future validation. The current experiments should be interpreted as proof-of-concept known-edge validation under the tested indirect-path and omitted-common-driver conditions, rather than comprehensive whole-brain validation.

**2. Computational cost, practical scalability, and intended use**

- **Acknowledged limitation and intended practical analysis scope.** The original submission explicitly stated that “computational cost may limit scalability to larger datasets” (Conclusion, Limitations); computational practicality for exhaustive whole-brain analysis was therefore not an intended claim. In response to the original request, we added comparative runtime and memory measurements to quantify this acknowledged limitation. We agree that Dual-flow is substantially more computationally demanding than the baseline methods, that exhaustive analysis requires \(N(N-1)\) directed-pair evaluations, and that GPU parallelization reduces wall-clock time without reducing total computation. These measurements confirm that the present implementation is intended for focused, prespecified circuit analysis—such as the 12 directed pairs in the tongue-motion experiment—rather than exhaustive analysis of all directed pairs among hundreds of ROIs.

- **Scientific value of focused EC analysis.** Lack of exhaustive whole-brain scalability does not preclude practical neuroimaging utility. Focused analysis of prespecified circuits is an established use case across EC frameworks. [Smith et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3260563/) (*Frontiers in Systems Neuroscience*, 2012) note that effective connections are often estimated among a small number of task-relevant ROIs. [Deshpande and Hu](https://doi.org/10.1089/brain.2012.0091) (*Brain Connectivity*, 2012) distinguish confirmatory circuit analysis from exploratory network discovery as established approaches serving different scientific objectives. Broad methodological reviews similarly distinguish hypothesis-driven analysis of predefined subnetworks from unrestricted whole-brain exploration ([Bielczyk et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6370462/), *Network Neuroscience*, 2019; [Rossini et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clinph.2019.06.006), *Clinical Neurophysiology*, 2019).

  Our empirical experiment follows this established hypothesis-driven design: four task-relevant tongue-motion ROIs were prespecified from activation maps and neuroanatomical evidence, restricting inference to 12 directed pairs. Within this intended setting, distribution-aware modeling of non-Gaussian residuals provides a distinct methodological capability even though the current implementation is not designed for unrestricted whole-brain discovery.

- **Future scalability.** We will state the intended scope more prominently and avoid implying current whole-brain computational practicality. Candidate-edge screening, amortized estimation, reuse of trained components, and further computational optimization remain important directions for extending the method to larger networks.

In summary, the present evidence supports known-edge recovery and practical use within the tested focused-circuit conditions. Prior whole-brain applications of the shared FIR backbone additionally demonstrate spatially coherent, data-driven temporal estimates across heterogeneous neuroanatomical regions. We agree, however, that the current study neither establishes nor was designed to establish the reliability or computational practicality of unrestricted whole-brain Dual-flow analysis. We will make this intended scope more explicit both in the Limitations paragraph of the Conclusion and in the new Appendix background section suggested by Reviewer T6Kn.
