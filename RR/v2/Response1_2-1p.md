For conditions derived from prior benchmarks, the cited topology was retained; all weight or topology modifications are explicitly reported. *In heterogeneous-HRF conditions, each ROI’s delay and transit time varied across realizations with SD=0.10 s and CV=5%, respectively; realized delays and transit times were bounded to ±1.50 s and 0.65–1.40 s. HRF variability was motivated by Handwerker et al. (Neuroimage, 2004) and Smith et al. (Neuroimage, 2011).

Using the Smith et al. (2011) simulation framework, we generated 50 independent stochastic realizations per condition under matched acquisition and noise settings rather than reusing released time series. Topology and hidden-driver manipulations differed across conditions; HRFs additionally varied across ROIs and realizations only in the heterogeneous-HRF conditions. All methods received identical data and ground truth. GIMME was not evaluated on Macq28 because 28 ROIs exceed its documented recommended range of 3–20 variables (Beltz and Gates, 2017). Performance is reported in Table X.
 
**Note on evaluation.** Our initial post-processing retained the dominant direction within each ROI pair before calculating metrics, analogous to the target in many unidirectional EC evaluations (Roebroeck et al., 2005; Smith et al., 2011). This suited the original nonreciprocal topologies but could not represent X->Y and Y->X simultaneously. We therefore evaluated every ordered edge independently, allowing zero, one, or both directions. For each realization, AUROC and AUPRC were computed from all N(N−1) ordered-edge scores. Precision, sensitivity, and FPR were computed on each held-out realization using a method-specific threshold selected from the other 49 realizations by maximizing Matthews correlation coefficient (MCC). The same procedure was applied to all available method-condition evaluations. Differences from the initial table reflect the expanded evaluation target and threshold calibration; estimator training and outputs were unchanged.
